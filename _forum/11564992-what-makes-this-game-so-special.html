Sorry for taking so long to respond,

I think that one of the main reasons is how easy it is to relate to Hype in the game. He only speaks twice and his generic appearence (due to being a playmobil character) and lack of any real facial features allows the gamer to easily immerse themself into the storyline and picture themselves as Hype. This gives the impression that they are actually in the game rather than simply playing it.

The richness of the supporting characters also plays a large part. The voice acting is first class and emotion is very well conveyed, (especially by whomever voiced Gogoud). For example, you get a sense of sadness and melancholy when Gogoud says his final goodbye to Hype; and even the minor characters portray emotion well, such as Wellet when he finally completes his boots after years of research and gives them to you.

Another reason why the game is so great is the pace of the storyline. The game is long enough to let the story flow while also short enough so that it doesn't get boring. For example if as soon as you got to the era of Taskan IV you had to fight Barnak straight away then it would be somewhat of an anticlimax after such a big build-up. On the flip side if the Black Tower featured a large labyrinth filled with dead-ends which took an hour to get through then the game would be quite dull and you would get bored very quickly.

A forth thing that contributes to the legendary status of the game (amongst us anyway) is both the build-up of tension and the progression of difficulty. At the start, the game is very calm and peaceful, and there are no real challenges with only wolves and boars to fight. The tension and difficulty both increase at a shallow but constant gradient and by the era of Taskan III both are nearing their peaks. Enost has betrayed the king to prepare for the imminent arrival of Hype's mortal enemy, the Black Knight; and Hype must face his antithesis for the toughest boss fight of the game. The discussion of the Black Knight ahead of his arrival creates a sense of foreboding and of panic and expectation as Barnak's appearance grows ever closer.

I'm sorry for the essay but I have so much to say about the game. Well done if you made it to the end. :)

I played Hype long ago as a kid; I've beat it countless times, and it still remains one of my favorite games. I'm playing it again for the first time in years, and I just haven't wanted to turn it off. The story is so captivating, the gameplay is simple, and it's been fun to play. Right now, I just finished retrieving the almanacs and am on my way back to Torras in the Era of Taskan IV.

What makes Hype special to me is that it was among te first video games that I had ever beaten (the other was Kingdom Hearts). Hype has held a place in my heart for as long as I can remember. The puzzles were tricky enough to stump an adult, but they were also simple enough that a child (who was the target audience anyway) could solve them. I remember the excitement I felt whenever I'd solve a puzzle or beat a boss I had been stuck on.

I also really enjoyed the music (especially the epic boss music). I find myself humming some of the tunes at work to help pass the time.
